## Interview guide

1. Walk through the most recent harvest. Who recorded its quantity, date and location, and where is that record now?
2. Follow its next handover. How did both parties agree on quantity and responsibility? How do they correct mistakes?
3. Ask how a laboratory report reaches the next buyer. Can the buyer check that it belongs to the same batch?
4. Ask how packaging links individual jars to a batch. What happens if a label is copied or a batch needs a recall?
5. Ask which task causes the most effort or disagreement, and request a concrete recent example if the participant is comfortable sharing it.
6. Check actual phone/browser availability, network outages, charging access, language preferences and who would operate a shared phone.
7. Explain the proposed approval step. Ask who should control a signing key and who should help if a phone is lost. Do not create a real wallet or request a private key during an exploratory interview.
8. Ask what would make the prototype useful enough to try, and what would prevent a trial.

Interview willing people in different roles. Record who declined or which roles were unavailable without identifying them publicly. State the actual sample and selection method in the submission; a convenient small sample is not evidence about all beekeepers.

## Prototype trial tasks

Use synthetic batches and test wallets before enrolling real users. Ask a participant to record a harvest, review an approval, find a laboratory report, check a jar and locate a recall. Record completion, errors, time and requests for help. A simulated sensor reading must retain its SIMULATED label. A failed network or missing report must stay visible.

For a later hardware trial, separately record sensor model, calibration method, placement, sample interval, power interruptions, phone/browser, offline duration, journal capacity, battery behavior and the number of packets observed versus uniquely stored. Verify the server-authenticated receipt before firmware deletes a reading. These trials are pending, not completed features or results.
