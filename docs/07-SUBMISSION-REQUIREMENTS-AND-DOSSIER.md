## Field 1 — Problem statement in simple language — required

Draft: Small beekeeper groups need an accessible way to connect a honey harvest to its laboratory evidence, packaging and subsequent handovers. Buyers often cannot inspect those records from a jar label, and a quality complaint can be difficult to trace back through the supply chain. Farmers also need timely information about hive conditions. Honey Chain combines assisted harvest recording, hive monitoring and inspectable batch evidence in one prototype.

The provided KVIC problem statement is the brief. Local prevalence and economic effects remain to be validated; blockchain itself cannot determine whether honey is adulterated.

## Field 6 — Existing situation/solutions and gaps — required

Draft framing for validation: paper logs, phone messages, spreadsheets and ordinary QR pages can record provenance, but independent parties may still need to resolve who approved an event, whether a report changed, and whether jar counts exceed the source lot. Farmer data entry and hardware connectivity can add adoption barriers. The team will compare the prototype with the actual beneficiary's current workflow and relevant existing systems before claiming a competitive advantage or patent novelty.

## Field 7 — Proposed solution and what is new — required

Honey Chain combines a familiar WhatsApp/cooperative interface with a shared provenance registry, batch-linked laboratory evidence, quantity-bounded packaging and consumer QR checks. IoT readings and disease/health analytics support hive management. A phone or gateway connects local field devices to a server through an MQTT uplink. Private data and high-volume sensor history remain off-chain; compact evidence commitments and accountable events belong on-chain.

The intended differentiator is practical access for small beekeeper clusters plus inspectable evidence and failure-aware verification. This is a product hypothesis requiring comparison and user validation, not a claim that blockchain traceability is new.
